European court: returning migrants who came via Belarus is not automatically a rights breach

The Grand Chamber of the European Court of Human Rights heard three cases against Poland, Latvia and Lithuania. States must first identify and protect vulnerable people.

24 November 2026 · 1 min read · California Uzbek

On 24 November 2026, the Grand Chamber of the European Court of Human Rights (ECtHR) issued a major ruling. It held that, in situations where migration is instrumentalised, returning migrants to Belarus does not in itself breach Article 3 of the Convention (the prohibition of torture and of inhuman or degrading treatment). However, the Court said states must first identify and protect children and other vulnerable people.

Which court and which cases

This is the Strasbourg-based European Court of Human Rights, which belongs to the Council of Europe; it is not the same as the EU Court of Justice. The Court heard three linked cases together: A.R. and Others v. Poland, H.M.M. and Others v. Latvia, and C.O.C.G. and Others v. Lithuania. The applicants were nationals of Afghanistan, Iraq (mainly Iraqi Kurds) and Cuba who tried to cross into Poland, Latvia and Lithuania via Belarus in 2021–2022 and were pushed back.

Findings for each state

Against Poland, the Court found a violation of Article 3: the first applicant was returned to Belarus on 20 October 2021 despite an interim Rule 39 measure barring his return. Poland was ordered to pay two Afghan applicants €20,000 and €5,000. Against Latvia, the Court found a violation for children and their family members returned to Belarus, but no violation for the non-vulnerable adult applicants. Against Lithuania, the Court found no violation by 11 votes to 6 — the applicants did not fall into a vulnerable category.

What 'instrumentalised migration' means

The Court defined the term as a third state encouraging, facilitating or forcing the movement of migrants in order to destabilise neighbouring states or the wider region. It refers to the situation that began in 2021, when the Belarusian government channelled Afghan, Iraqi and Cuban migrants toward the EU's eastern borders. The Court said such pressure was a deliberate threat to the integrity of a state's borders.
