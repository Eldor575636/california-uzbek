How to Send Money to Uzbekistan From Abroad: Methods, Costs and Rules

A practical reference based on the World Bank's Remittance Prices Worldwide reports, Central Bank of Uzbekistan data and reputable news sources. It explains the main transfer methods, the true cost (fee plus exchange-rate margin), speed, receiving rules and safety tips. This is informational, not financial advice.

6 October 2026 · 3 min read · California Uzbek

For Uzbeks abroad, sending money home is the most common financial task. How much actually arrives depends not only on the advertised fee but also on a hidden difference in the exchange rate. That is why choosing the right method and comparing the total cost matters.

The main ways to send money to Uzbekistan

There are four practical channels: (1) money-transfer operators and apps — such as Western Union, MoneyGram, Wise, Remitly, Paysend; (2) bank wire (SWIFT); (3) card-to-card transfers — via Visa/Mastercard to Uzcard or Humo cards; (4) cash pickup through an operator's agents. On the Russia corridor, the Golden Crown (Zolotaya Korona / KoronaPay) system is dominant.

The scale of remittances to Uzbekistan

According to Central Bank data, in 2024 Uzbekistan received $14.8 billion in remittances (up 29.8% year on year). Of that, Russia accounted for $11.5 billion (about 77%), the USA for $577 million (+35%), South Korea for $534 million and Kazakhstan for $795 million. In 2025 the total reached $18.9 billion (+27.2%), with Russia's share holding at roughly 78%.

The true cost: two parts

The true cost of a transfer has TWO parts: (1) the visible fee; (2) the exchange-rate margin — the hidden gap between the market rate and the rate you are offered. Often the main cost sits in that hidden margin, so don't rely on a "zero fee" ad alone — calculate the total cost.

According to the World Bank, in the third quarter of 2025 the global average cost of sending money was 6.36% of the amount sent. Digital (online) transfers averaged 4.59%, while cash/agent transfers averaged 7.30% — meaning digital methods are usually cheaper. Under the UN Sustainable Development Goal, this cost should fall below 3% by 2030.

Speed

Cash pickup and card-to-card transfers usually complete within minutes. A bank wire (SWIFT) typically takes 1–3 business days (longer if correspondent banks are involved).

How the money is received in Uzbekistan

A transfer in foreign currency can be received by the recipient in dollars (cash) or converted to soʻm. Uzcard and Humo are the national card systems; they work only inside Uzbekistan and in soʻm, so an international transfer lands on these cards converted to soʻm. Mobile wallets such as Payme, Click and Uzum are also widely used. Currency operations are regulated by the Central Bank of Uzbekistan (cbu.uz); since the soʻm was floated to a market rate in September 2017, the rate is set on a market basis.

Rules and limits

To collect a transfer or cash, the recipient needs an identity document (passport), and for cash pickup the transfer's secret reference number (for example, the MTCN). Carrying large amounts of foreign cash across the border requires a customs declaration — because the current thresholds change from time to time, verify the exact amount on official customs or Central Bank sources.

Safety tips

Compare the TOTAL cost (fee + exchange-rate margin), not just the advertised fee. Use digital methods where possible — they are usually cheaper. Use only licensed, official operators; avoid sending cash via "informal" couriers. Keep the receipt and reference number until the recipient confirms they have received the money. Enter the recipient's name exactly as on their ID and the correct card type (Uzcard or Humo). On the Russia corridor, bear in mind that some systems may be temporarily unavailable due to sanctions.

Note: this article provides general information and is not financial advice. Fees, rates and rules vary by operator and over time — check current terms before sending.
