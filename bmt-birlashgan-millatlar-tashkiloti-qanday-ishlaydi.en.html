How the United Nations (UN) works: structure, the Security Council and Uzbekistan's membership

The UN was founded in 1945 and today unites 193 member states. Here is how the General Assembly, the Security Council, the veto, the Secretary-General and the main agencies work — and how Uzbekistan has taken part since 1992.

23 November 2026 · 4 min read · California Uzbek

The United Nations (UN) is the world's largest international organization, created after the Second World War to maintain international peace and security and to develop cooperation among states. The UN Charter was signed on 26 June 1945 in San Francisco by 50 nations and entered into force on 24 October 1945 — which is why 24 October is marked today as UN Day. The organization had 51 original members, and its headquarters is in New York City (Source: un.org — UN Charter; Wikipedia — Charter of the United Nations).

The six principal organs

The UN has six principal organs. The General Assembly is the main deliberative and policymaking body, where all member states are represented. The Security Council has primary responsibility for maintaining international peace and security. The Economic and Social Council (ECOSOC) coordinates economic, social and related work. The Trusteeship Council oversaw territories moving toward self-government, but suspended operations on 1 November 1994 after Palau, the last trust territory, became independent. The International Court of Justice (ICJ) is the principal judicial organ and is the only organ not located in New York — it sits at the Peace Palace in The Hague, Netherlands. The Secretariat carries out the day-to-day work and is headed by the Secretary-General (Source: un.org — Main Bodies; Britannica — United Nations: Principal organs).

The General Assembly: one vote per state

The General Assembly currently has 193 member states, and each has a single, equal vote regardless of size, population or economic power. Under Article 18 of the Charter, the most important questions — such as peace and security, admitting or expelling members, and the budget — require a two-thirds majority of those present and voting; most other matters need a simple majority (Source: un.org — General Assembly; Wikipedia — United Nations General Assembly resolution).

The Security Council and the veto

The Security Council has 15 members: 5 permanent and 10 non-permanent. The permanent five — China, France, Russia, the United Kingdom and the United States — hold the veto. The 10 non-permanent members are elected by the General Assembly for two-year terms and have no veto. Substantive (non-procedural) resolutions require 9 affirmative votes, including no opposing vote from any of the five permanent members — so any one of the permanent five can block (veto) such a resolution (Source: un.org — Security Council current members; Britannica — UN Security Council).

The Secretary-General

The Secretary-General is the UN's chief administrative officer and heads the Secretariat. The term is five years, renewable, and by convention limited to two terms. Since 2017 the office has been held by the organization's 9th Secretary-General, António Guterres of Portugal; his second term ends at the close of 2026 (Source: un.org — About the Secretary-General; Britannica — António Guterres).

Main agencies and programmes

The UN system includes many specialized agencies, funds and programmes. UNICEF protects children's rights, survival and development. UNHCR is the refugee agency, protecting refugees and displaced people. The World Health Organization (WHO) handles international public health. UNESCO promotes cooperation in education, science and culture. The UN Development Programme (UNDP) fights poverty and inequality. The World Food Programme (WFP) is on the front line against hunger and won the 2020 Nobel Peace Prize (Source: un.org — The UN System; UNHCR — The UN and international institutions).

Uzbekistan and the UN

Uzbekistan joined the UN on 2 March 1992, about six months after independence — the same day as Armenia, Azerbaijan, Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan, Moldova, Tajikistan and Turkmenistan. The UN office in Tashkent opened in 1993; today around 25 UN agencies, funds and programmes operate in the country. Uzbekistan was elected for the first time to the UN Human Rights Council for 2021–2023 on 13 October 2020. At Uzbekistan's initiative, the UN Multi-Partner Human Security Trust Fund for the Aral Sea Region was established in November 2018 (Source: uzbekistan.un.org; un.int — Uzbekistan and the UN; UN News, 2018).

How the budget is formed

The UN is funded chiefly by members' mandatory assessed contributions. The amount is set by a 'scale of assessments' negotiated every three years, based on each country's capacity to pay (gross national income), with a floor of 0.001% and a ceiling of 22%. The United States is the single largest contributor to the regular budget, at the maximum 22% rate (Source: Congressional Research Service — U.S. Funding to the UN System; Wikipedia — United States and the United Nations).

Why this matters

Knowing the UN's structure helps make sense of international news: it explains why a Security Council veto can halt certain decisions, how General Assembly resolutions are adopted, and where Uzbekistan stands within the system. This article presents only verified dates and figures; it offers no judgments or interpretation.
