The British Council marks 30 years in Uzbekistan: 1.7 million participants and about ten UK universities

The British Council has operated in Uzbekistan since 1996. Over 30 years its programmes have reached 1.7 million direct participants, and its initiatives have reached 42 million people. Today more than 10,000 students earn a British diploma inside Uzbekistan.

12 November 2026 · 1 min read · California Uzbek

The British Council — the United Kingdom's official organisation for cultural relations and education — has marked 30 years of work in Uzbekistan. Gazeta.uz reported this on 12 November. The organisation has operated in the country since 1996; over that period 1.7 million people have taken part directly in its programmes, and its campaigns and initiatives have reached more than 42 million residents of the two countries.

UK universities in Uzbekistan

Today more than 10,000 students in Uzbekistan earn a UK higher-education diploma without leaving the country. About ten British institutions operate there; the first was Westminster International University in Tashkent, opened in 2002 with British Council involvement. Demand for UK education is growing.

New projects

To mark the anniversary the organisation announced new education initiatives: a 'Women in STEM' scholarship (winners receive a one-year master's place at UK universities), a programme to support vocational education, and research on 'The Economic Value of English'. The British Council also runs English-language exams, teacher training and cultural-exchange projects in Uzbekistan.

The director's comment

Michael Holgate, director of the British Council in Uzbekistan, said the anniversary prompts the organisation to look back while also considering what it wants to preserve for the future. 'Our task is to help people get to know one another,' he said. An exhibition titled 'Who are we?', dedicated to the 30th anniversary, also opened in Tashkent.
