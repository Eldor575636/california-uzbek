Iran says it shot down a US drone over the Strait of Hormuz; the claim is unverified

Iran's Revolutionary Guards said on 20 October they intercepted a US MQ-9 drone over the Strait of Hormuz — its claimed 54th US drone. The US did not confirm it; CENTCOM says the strait stays open to trade.

20 October 2026 · 2 min read · California Uzbek

Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) announced on 20 October 2026 that it had intercepted and destroyed a US MQ-9 'Reaper' drone over the Strait of Hormuz. The statement claimed it was the 54th US drone Iran has downed. The news was carried by Iran's state and semi-official media, Fars and Tasnim. The claim has not been independently verified.

What Iran claimed

According to the IRGC statement, the drone was tracked and destroyed early Tuesday over the Strait of Hormuz by a 'new, advanced air defence system' of the IRGC Aerospace Force. Iranian media called the aircraft one belonging to the 'US terrorist military' and said the interception took place under the control of the country's integrated air defence network.

The claim is unverified

Outlets such as Middle East Eye and Arab Times stressed that the claim has not been independently verified. The US did not officially confirm the loss of a drone. Throughout the 2026 war both sides have issued one-sided statements about their military 'successes'; without independent evidence such figures and claims cannot be checked.

The US position

CENTCOM spokesman US Navy Capt. Tim Hawkins told Fox News that about 97% of ships seeking to transit the Strait of Hormuz had done so successfully without being hit by an Iranian projectile, and that the strait remains open to commercial shipping. CENTCOM has previously said it struck multiple targets to degrade Iran's ability to attack shipping.

Hormuz and the war backdrop

The crisis began on 28 February 2026 after US and Israeli strikes on Iran; Iran then tried to close the Strait of Hormuz and threatened passing vessels. The Strait of Hormuz is a narrow waterway through which roughly 25% of the world's seaborne oil trade passes. At the peak of the crisis Brent crude rose from $100 a barrel on 8 March to a high of $126. By October more than 100 ship incidents had been recorded in the strait, and the situation remains unstable.

Why it matters for Uzbekistan

Tension in the Strait of Hormuz affects world oil prices, and oil prices in turn can indirectly affect Uzbekistan through fuel and the wider economy. The episode also shows why wartime claims — whoever makes them — should be weighed against independent evidence before being accepted.
