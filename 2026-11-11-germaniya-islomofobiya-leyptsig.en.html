One in three Germans backs anti-Muslim statements, Leipzig study finds

The University of Leipzig's 2026 Authoritarianism Study: anti-Muslim attitudes reach 34.1%, right-wing extremism has doubled, and satisfaction with democracy is at the lowest level in the survey's history.

11 November 2026 · 1 min read · California Uzbek

Anti-Muslim attitudes are rising in Germany. According to the 2026 Authoritarianism Study by the Else-Frenkel-Brunswik Institute (EFBI) at the University of Leipzig, 34.1% of the population agrees with anti-Muslim statements — up from 33.2% in 2024 and 26.5% in 2022. In eastern Germany the share is 41.6%, in the west 32.2%. The study was published by the Heinrich Böll Foundation and presented at a press conference in Berlin on 11 November.

The key figures

The share of people holding a closed right-wing extremist worldview has grown from 4.5% to 6.9% in two years. About 58% of them say they would vote for the Alternative for Germany (AfD). Xenophobia and national chauvinism have also reached their highest levels since the survey began.

At the same time, satisfaction with how democracy actually functions has fallen to 33.3% (22.8% in the east) — down from 42.3% in 2024 and the lowest in the survey's history. Support for democracy as an idea fell below 90% (to 89.3%) for the first time.

How it was done

The survey was carried out between 16 March and 3 June 2026 among 2,507 respondents, drawn as a random sample by an independent institute. The series has run since 2002. Importantly, the study measures people's attitudes and levels of agreement — not the number of actual hate crimes or incidents.

While Anadolu Agency framed its report around anti-Muslim sentiment, the underlying study is broader — covering authoritarianism, antisemitism and trust in democracy; the anti-Muslim figure itself is confirmed by the German-language primary sources.
