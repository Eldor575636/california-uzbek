How to vote in the United States: registering and voting for new citizens

To vote in the U.S. you must first register (except in North Dakota). Only U.S. citizens who are 18 by Election Day and residents of their state may vote. A newly naturalized citizen can register the day of the ceremony. There are four ways to register: online, by mail (the National Mail Voter Registration Form), in person, or at the DMV (the 1993 National Voter Registration Act — "motor voter"). Deadlines vary by state. Based on official sources (vote.gov, USCIS, the Election Assistance Commission).

7 November 2026 · 3 min read · California Uzbek

Uzbeks who gain U.S. citizenship often ask the same question: how do I vote now? Voting is a core right of a U.S. citizen, but the process varies by state and almost everywhere requires registering first. The following explains the process using only official sources — facts only.

Who can vote

To vote in federal and state elections three basic conditions must be met: the voter must be a U.S. citizen, be 18 by Election Day, and reside in the state where they vote. Non-citizens — including lawful permanent residents (green-card holders) — cannot vote in federal and most state and local elections.

Once a citizen, you can register right away

From the day you take the Oath of Allegiance at the naturalization ceremony, a new citizen may register to vote. Registration forms are handed out at many ceremonies. According to USCIS, you must not register or vote before you are a citizen — doing so can even harm your citizenship case.

Four ways to register

You can register in four ways: (1) online on your state's election website; (2) by mail — filling out and sending the National Mail Voter Registration Form; (3) in person — at the state election office or designated government agencies; (4) when you apply for or renew a driver's license (known as "motor voter"). The motor-voter rule was created by the National Voter Registration Act (NVRA), signed on 20 May 1993.

The national mail form does not work in every state

The National Mail Voter Registration Form is used in most U.S. states, but four states — New Hampshire, North Dakota, Wisconsin and Wyoming — do not accept it and have their own procedures. North Dakota keeps no voter registration at all — a citizen can vote directly with valid identification.

Deadlines vary by state

In most states the last day to register is about 30 days before the election. But some states allow same-day registration on Election Day itself. In the U.S., the federal Election Day is the first Tuesday after the first Monday in November. Check the exact deadline and rules on your own state's election office website.

Three ways to cast a ballot

Once registered, there are three main ways to vote: before the election at early-voting sites; on Election Day at your polling place; or by mail with an absentee/mail ballot. Which options exist depends on the state: some offer mail voting to everyone, others require a reason for it.

ID the first time you vote

Under federal law, if you registered by mail and are voting for the first time in your state, you may be required to show identification: a current photo ID, or a utility bill, bank statement, government check or government document showing your name and address. Many states have expanded ID requirements, so learn your state's rule in advance.

An important warning

A non-citizen must not register or vote. Even as a lawful permanent resident, you have no right to vote in federal elections until you naturalize; improperly registering or voting can seriously damage a future citizenship or immigration case. If in doubt, seek advice before voting.

Why this matters for the diaspora

The Uzbek community in the U.S. is growing, and each year thousands of compatriots naturalize. Voting — from a local school board to Congress and the presidency — is the legal way for the community's voice to be heard. Registration is free and takes a few minutes; the key is not to miss the deadline.
