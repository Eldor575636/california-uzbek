Two disasters in one day: at least 21 die off Crete, 39 off Senegal

At least 21 migrants died off southern Crete, Greece, while at least 39 died when a boat bound for Spain's Canary Islands sank off Senegal.

9 November 2026 · 1 min read · California Uzbek

Two disasters were recorded on Europe-bound sea routes within a single day. At least 21 migrants died off Greece's island of Crete, and at least 39 people died when a boat bound for Spain's Canary Islands sank off the coast of Senegal.

Crete, Greece

According to the Hellenic Coast Guard, more than 100 people were rescued in several operations off southern Crete; most survivors were reported to be Sudanese. The BBC had initially reported 13 dead and 18 rescued from one boat; the overall toll was later raised to 21 across two shipwrecks.

Off Senegal

According to the Senegalese government, the boat set off from Saint-Louis in the north of the country and sank near the Kébémer area. The victims included women and children; the passengers came from Senegal, Gambia, Guinea-Bissau, Guinea and Nigeria. Senegalese President Bassirou Diomaye Faye offered condolences and said state resources had been mobilised for the search and rescue.

The bigger picture

According to the International Organization for Migration (IOM), more than 7,600 migrants died or went missing worldwide in 2025 — about 21 people a day. The Mediterranean and the Atlantic (Canary Islands) routes are among the most dangerous.
