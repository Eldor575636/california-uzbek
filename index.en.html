Uzbekistan joins the CIS: the Alma-Ata Protocol (1991)

On 21 December 1991, in Alma-Ata (Almaty), Kazakhstan, 11 former Soviet republics signed the Alma-Ata Protocol and Declaration, formally establishing the Commonwealth of Independent States (CIS). The documents declared that the USSR 'ceases to exist as a subject of international law and geopolitical reality.' For Uzbekistan, the protocol was signed by President Islam Karimov. It was the first multilateral framework after independence. Based only on verified facts.

Getting a U.S. driver's license (DMV): how it works

In the U.S. a driver's license is issued not by the federal government but by each state's motor-vehicle agency (DMV), so the rules differ from state to state. The common steps are: documents, a written (knowledge) test, a learner's permit, and a road test. From 7 May 2025 the REAL ID requirement is in force — a federally compliant license or a passport is needed to board a domestic flight or enter certain federal buildings. Based on official sources.

The Uzbek diaspora in China (East Turkestan): how it formed

The Uzbeks of China are a small but ancient community that has lived for centuries in Xinjiang (East Turkestan). Most descend from merchants who came along the Silk Road from Bukhara, Kokand, Andijan and Samarkand. The community began settling in Xinjiang's cities from the 16th century and grew further after the 18th century. Today they are one of China's 56 officially recognized ethnic groups: the 2020 census counted 12,742. Based only on verified facts.

How the Central Bank of Uzbekistan and monetary policy work

The central bank is the state's bank of issue: it issues the national currency (the som), regulates banks, and runs monetary policy. Founded in 1991; a new law of 11 November 2019 strengthened its independence and set price stability as its primary objective. In January 2020 it adopted an inflation-targeting regime with a medium-term target of 5%. Its main tool is the policy rate: held at 14% since March 2025 (unchanged again at the 16 September 2026 meeting). The som was floated on 5 September 2017. Based on official Central Bank data.

The Uzbek diaspora in Germany and Europe: how it formed

The Uzbek community in Europe — especially in Germany — is younger and smaller than those in Russia and the US, but it is growing. Mid-20th century: Turkestani political émigrés; after independence: students and professionals; from 2015: asylum seekers. A key turning point: on 15 September 2024 Germany and Uzbekistan signed a migration and mobility partnership in Samarkand. In 2024 Germany issued the most EU Blue Cards — 56,252.
