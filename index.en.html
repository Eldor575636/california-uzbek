The Volga (Kazan) Tatars in Uzbekistan: how a merchant and educator community took shape

The Volga (Kazan) Tatars are part of Uzbekistan's centuries-old multi-ethnic society. They came to Turkestan mainly in the 19th century, under Tsarist Russia, as merchants, interpreters, teachers and mullahs, acting as trade and cultural intermediaries. Their numbers grew in the Soviet era, including through the 1941–1945 wartime evacuation: the 1979 census recorded 531,205 Tatars, and the 1989 census 467,829 (2.4% of the population). After independence many emigrated to Russia, and by 2017 the number had fallen to about 195,000. This is a community distinct from the Crimean Tatars, a separate people deported here in 1944. The U.S. court system and jury duty: the basics for immigrants

The U.S. has two parallel court systems: the federal courts (94 district courts → 13 courts of appeals → the Supreme Court) and each of the 50 states' own courts. A criminal case is brought by the government; a civil case is usually a private dispute. The Constitution guarantees the right to a jury. To serve on a federal jury you must be a U.S. CITIZEN: green-card or visa holders cannot serve. If you are summoned by mistake, do not ignore it — fill out the form and indicate you are not a citizen. A reference — facts only, not legal advice.

How investing in gold works: forms, pricing, the halal rules and zakat

Gold is a familiar store of savings for many families. There are several ways to own it: physical bars and coins, jewellery, an allocated or unallocated bank account, a gold ETF, mining stocks and futures. The gold price is set on the international spot market, quoted per troy ounce (31.1035 grams) in US dollars. Gold pays no income (no interest or dividend) and its price can fall. In Islam gold is a "ribawi" commodity: it must be traded spot, hand-to-hand. The zakat nisab for gold is 85 grams of pure gold, at a rate of 2.5%. A reference — facts only, not financial advice.
