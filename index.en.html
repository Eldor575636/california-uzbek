How investing in gold works: forms, pricing, the halal rules and zakat

Gold is a familiar store of savings for many families. There are several ways to own it: physical bars and coins, jewellery, an allocated or unallocated bank account, a gold ETF, mining stocks and futures. The gold price is set on the international spot market, quoted per troy ounce (31.1035 grams) in US dollars. Gold pays no income (no interest or dividend) and its price can fall. In Islam gold is a "ribawi" commodity: it must be traded spot, hand-to-hand. The zakat nisab for gold is 85 grams of pure gold, at a rate of 2.5%. A reference — facts only, not financial advice.

Bukharan students sent to Germany (1922): the Jadids' dream of knowledge, and the purge

In 1922 the government of the Bukharan People's Soviet Republic sent a group of young people to Germany for higher education. In all, about 70 students from Bukhara and Turkestan were sent to Germany; roughly 56 of them were from Bukhara, and 44 were placed in Berlin schools that summer. The initiative is linked mainly to the education minister (later prime minister) Abdurauf Fitrat. After the republic was abolished in 1924 the students were recalled; in the 1930s purges many were shot or exiled as "nationalists" or under "foreign influence," while some remained abroad as émigrés. The TRACECA transport corridor (1993–1998): independent Uzbekistan's search for routes to the world

TRACECA — the "Transport Corridor Europe–Caucasus–Asia" — is a programme launched by the European Union at a conference in Brussels in May 1993, aimed at developing a route connecting Central Asia to Europe across the Caspian, the Caucasus and the Black Sea, bypassing Russian territory. Uzbekistan was one of the programme's eight founding states. On 8 September 1998 the Basic Multilateral Agreement (MLA) was signed in Baku; 12 states adopted the document and a permanent secretariat was based in Baku. In the first years of independence this was part of Uzbekistan's policy of diversifying its transport routes. History — facts only.

Qard hasan: how a benevolent, interest-free loan works in Islam

Qard hasan — Arabic for "a beautiful loan" — is an interest-free, benevolent loan given to someone in need in Islam. The borrower repays only the principal received; no interest or markup is taken, because that would be riba. The Qur'an mentions the concept in several verses (for example al-Baqara 2:245, al-Hadid 57:11, al-Taghabun 64:17). Qard hasan differs from charity: charity is not repaid, but a loan is. Ways to move money in the US: how ACH, bank wire, Zelle and Venmo work

In the US there are several ways to move money between accounts, and they differ in speed, cost and whether they can be reversed. ACH transfers move in batches, usually in 1–3 business days, cheaply or free, and can be reversed; a bank wire (Fedwire) moves individually, same day, but for a $25–50 fee and with no reversal. Zelle works bank-to-bank in minutes, while Venmo and PayPal hold money in an app balance and move it to your bank over ACH. A reference — facts only.
