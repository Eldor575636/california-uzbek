International document authentication: apostille, consular legalization, notarization and certified translation

To use a document (a diploma, a birth or marriage certificate, a power of attorney) officially abroad, it must be authenticated. Among parties to the 1961 Hague Convention this is an apostille: a single certificate that recognizes the signature and seal and replaces the multi-step consular legalization. For Uzbekistan the Hague Convention entered into force on 15 April 2012 (the instrument of accession was deposited on 25 July 2011); the USA has been a party since 1981 — so between Uzbekistan and the USA an apostille is sufficient, with no additional consular legalization required. Based on official sources.

Financial aid for U.S. higher education: FAFSA, grants, scholarships and student loans

Eligibility for federal aid for U.S. college and university study is determined through one application — the FAFSA (Free Application for Federal Student Aid). The FAFSA is free. From the 2024–25 award year it was simplified: the Student Aid Index (SAI) replaced the Expected Family Contribution (EFC). Main federal aid: the Pell Grant (maximum $7,395 for 2024–25 and 2025–26), FSEOG, Federal Work-Study and Direct Loans. Grants are not repaid; loans are repaid with interest. U.S. citizens, permanent residents (green card) and certain eligible non-citizens (refugees, asylees, parolees) qualify for federal aid; F-1 and undocumented students generally cannot get federal aid. The Tashkent Collective Security Treaty (15 May 1992) and Uzbekistan

On 15 May 1992 the Collective Security Treaty (CST) was signed in Tashkent — the organization that became the CSTO (ODKB) grew out of it. The six original signatories were Russia, Armenia, Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan, Tajikistan and Uzbekistan. The treaty entered into force on 20 April 1994 for a five-year term. Uzbekistan declined to renew it in 1999, rejoined in 2006, and suspended its membership in 2012. A foreign-policy concept adopted in 2012 set out non-participation in military blocs, no foreign military bases, and no sending troops abroad. Based on verified facts.

Uzbek labor migration to the Persian Gulf: the UAE, Qatar and Saudi Arabia (2017–2026)

The Persian Gulf states — the United Arab Emirates, Qatar and Saudi Arabia — are a new, still small but growing labor-migration destination for Uzbekistan. It began taking shape after 2017, within a policy of reducing over-reliance on Russia and diversifying markets. Organized recruitment rose from 35,800 people in 2022 to 161,800 in 2024; of that, recruitment to the UAE rose from 18 people in 2023 to 319 in 2024 (official data). In December 2024 an agreement to send medical staff to Saudi Arabia was signed. The corridor's scale is still far smaller than Russia (~60%). Based on official sources.
