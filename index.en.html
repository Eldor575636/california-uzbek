How to vote in the United States: registering and voting for new citizens

To vote in the U.S. you must first register (except in North Dakota). Only U.S. citizens who are 18 by Election Day and residents of their state may vote. A newly naturalized citizen can register the day of the ceremony. There are four ways to register: online, by mail (the National Mail Voter Registration Form), in person, or at the DMV (the 1993 National Voter Registration Act — "motor voter"). Deadlines vary by state. Based on official sources (vote.gov, USCIS, the Election Assistance Commission).

How OPEC works and how oil prices are set

OPEC — the Organization of the Petroleum Exporting Countries — was founded on 14 September 1960 in Baghdad by five states (Iran, Iraq, Kuwait, Saudi Arabia and Venezuela); its headquarters has been in Vienna since 1 September 1965. As of 2026 it has 11 member states (the United Arab Emirates left in 2026). OPEC members hold about 79.5% of the world's proven oil reserves and about 38% of production (2022). The group influences the market through production quotas; since 2016 it coordinates with countries such as Russia and Kazakhstan in the "OPEC+" format. Based on official sources.
