Uzbekistan identifies 12 human rights challenges linked to business activities

At its first National Dialogue on Business and Human Rights, an assessment presented by the National Human Rights Centre and UNDP identified 12 key challenges including labour rights, workplace safety, informal employment and environmental protection.

13 November 2026 · 1 min read · California Uzbek

Uzbekistan has held its first National Dialogue on Business and Human Rights. A national assessment presented at the event identified 12 key human rights challenges linked to business activities. Daryo and UzDaily reported this, citing the United Nations Development Programme (UNDP).

What challenges?

Among the challenges explicitly named in the sources are labour rights, occupational (workplace) safety, informal employment and environmental protection. They were identified through a National Baseline Assessment, which found that despite an improved legal framework, gaps remain in practical implementation and enforcement, with attention to risks in agriculture, construction and manufacturing.

Who organised it?

The dialogue was held in Tashkent on 9 November by Uzbekistan's National Human Rights Centre together with UNDP. It is based on the UN Guiding Principles on Business and Human Rights; discussions focused on developing a National Action Plan (NAP) on business and human rights. Training sessions for companies, banks and state bodies were held on 10–13 November. Small and medium-sized enterprises account for 85% of active companies in the country.

Officials' comments

Akmal Saidov, Director of the National Human Rights Centre, said: 'Uzbekistan has made significant progress in strengthening the protection of human rights. As our country continues its economic transformation and integration into global markets, it is increasingly important to ensure that business growth goes hand in hand with respect for human rights.' Akiko Fujii, UNDP Resident Representative in Uzbekistan, stressed that respecting human rights is 'not a burden on business, but a foundation for sustainable growth, trust and long-term success'.
