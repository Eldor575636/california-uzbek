How investing in gold works: forms, pricing, the halal rules and zakat

Gold is a familiar store of savings for many families. There are several ways to own it: physical bars and coins, jewellery, an allocated or unallocated bank account, a gold ETF, mining stocks and futures. The gold price is set on the international spot market, quoted per troy ounce (31.1035 grams) in US dollars. Gold pays no income (no interest or dividend) and its price can fall. In Islam gold is a "ribawi" commodity: it must be traded spot, hand-to-hand. The zakat nisab for gold is 85 grams of pure gold, at a rate of 2.5%. A reference — facts only, not financial advice.

9 November 2026 · 4 min read · California Uzbek

Gold has served as savings and a store of value for centuries. Today it can be owned in several ways, and its price is formed on the international market. The following explains, facts only, the forms of owning gold, how the price is set, its characteristics, the Islamic rules on trading it, and zakat on gold. This reference is not financial advice and offers no price prediction.

Physical gold: bars, coins, jewellery

The most familiar form is physical gold: bars, mint-issued coins and jewellery. Bars from an LBMA-approved refiner carry a serial number. Jewellery is usually an alloy (purity is measured in carats; pure gold is 24 carat), so only the pure-gold content is counted for valuation and zakat, excluding other metals and gemstones.

Gold on account: allocated and unallocated

Gold can also be held on account rather than physically. In an allocated account specific bars are set aside in your name and you fully own them; this is protected from the provider's insolvency. In an unallocated account you hold a contractual claim against the provider rather than specific bars — you are an unsecured creditor and are exposed if the provider cannot pay.

Gold ETFs, mining stocks and futures

A physically-backed gold ETF (for example a grantor-trust fund) holds real bars in vaults and its shares are valued against the LBMA gold price; however, owning an ETF share does not give you title to specific bars. Gold mining stocks are shares in companies that mine gold — exposure to the company (its operations and costs), not the metal directly. Gold futures are exchange contracts to buy or sell gold at a set price in the future — a deferred, not immediate, exchange of metal and money.

How the gold price is set

Gold trades on an international "spot" price, usually quoted per troy ounce in US dollars. One troy ounce is 31.1035 grams (more precisely 31.1034768 g); it is the official unit for precious metals and differs from the ordinary (avoirdupois) ounce of 28.35 g. The benchmark price is the "LBMA Gold Price," set by an electronic auction held twice each business day (10:30 and 15:00 London time).

What drives the gold price

Several factors influence the gold price (not a prediction, only observed relationships): inflation and currency weakness, interest rates (lower real yields have historically supported gold), central-bank gold buying, geopolitical uncertainty and reserve diversification, and the state of the US dollar (a weaker dollar makes gold cheaper for holders of other currencies).

Gold pays no income and its price can fall

Gold is often described as a long-term store of value and an inflation hedge. But it pays no income such as interest or a dividend, and its price is volatile — it can also fall depending on the factors above. So gold is not a guaranteed profit but an asset whose price fluctuates.

The Islamic rules on trading gold

In Islamic law gold is a "ribawi" commodity — like money, it is subject to the rules against riba. A hadith states: "Gold for gold, silver for silver... like for like, hand-to-hand (spot)" (Bukhari and Muslim). When the same type is exchanged it must be equal measure and spot; when the types differ the amounts may be unequal, but it must still be spot, hand-to-hand, without deferment. On 5 December 2016 AAOIFI (the Accounting and Auditing Organization for Islamic Financial Institutions), in collaboration with the World Gold Council, adopted Shari'ah Standard No. 57, "Gold and its Trading Controls" — the first standard governing gold as an investment.

Why futures and unallocated gold are questioned

Some scholars question gold futures, CFDs and unallocated gold because they lack immediate possession (qabd). A future is a deferred (forward) sale: gold and money do not change hands in the same session. Unallocated gold is a general claim (a debt), not ownership of specific bars. CFDs and leveraged products confer no ownership at all — only price exposure. AAOIFI Standard No. 57 requires either specific serial-numbered bars allocated to you, or a certificate issued on the trade day (T+0) allowing delivery on demand.

Zakat on gold

The zakat nisab for gold is 85 grams of pure gold (equated in sources to 20 mithqals). If gold reaches the nisab and a full lunar (Hijri) year (about 354 days) has passed over it, zakat of 2.5% (a quarter of a tenth) of its value is due. For alloyed or carat gold only the pure-gold content counts: pure gold = weight × (carat ÷ 24).

Why this matters for Uzbekistan and the diaspora

Saving in gold is common for families in Uzbekistan and the diaspora. The form of ownership (physical, on account, or an exchange product) determines its risk, liquidity and religious soundness. This reference explains the general facts; for a specific investment decision or a Shari'ah question, consulting a qualified specialist and a trusted religious body is advised.
