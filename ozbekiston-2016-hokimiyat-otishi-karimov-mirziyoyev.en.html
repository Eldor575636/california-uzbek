The 2016 transition of power: the death of Islam Karimov and the rise of Shavkat Mirziyoyev

On 2 September 2016 the death of Uzbekistan's first president, Islam Karimov, was announced — the only leader to have ruled the country for more than a quarter-century. Within weeks Prime Minister Shavkat Mirziyoyev became first interim and then elected president, and the country stepped into a new era of reform. This historical guide sets out the 2016 transition — the death, the constitutional succession question, the December election and the reforms that followed — with exact dates, based solely on reputable sources (Reuters/RFE-RL, TASS, Al Jazeera, gazeta.uz, European Parliament).

21 November 2026 · 3 min read · California Uzbek

The year 2016 was one of the most important turning points in Uzbekistan's history of independence: the first president, Islam Karimov, who had led the country since 1991, died, and power passed to a new leader, Shavkat Mirziyoyev. Below, this transition is set out with exact dates, based solely on reputable sources.

The death of Islam Karimov

Islam Karimov was hospitalized in Tashkent on 27 August 2016 after a brain hemorrhage (stroke); his illness was first publicly announced on 29 August by his daughter, Lola Karimova-Tillyaeva. His death was officially announced on 2 September 2016. Karimov was 78 (born 30 January 1938). He had been First Secretary of the Communist Party of Uzbekistan from 1989, president of the Uzbek SSR from 24 March 1990, and the first president of independent Uzbekistan from 1 September 1991 — the country's only president until his death. He was buried in Samarkand, at the Hazrati Khizr complex, on 3 September 2016.

The constitutional succession question

Under the constitution, the duties of president should have been temporarily performed by the chairman of the Senate — at that time Nigmatilla Yuldashev. However, on 8 September 2016 a joint session of both chambers of the Oliy Majlis appointed Prime Minister Shavkat Mirziyoyev as interim (acting) president. According to the official account, Yuldashev himself proposed Mirziyoyev's candidacy, citing his "many years of experience in state government" and his "respect among the population." (The stated reasons rest on the official statement; independent outlets described these days as a "behind-the-scenes power game.")

Who is Shavkat Mirziyoyev

Shavkat Mirziyoyev was born on 24 July 1957. He had served as prime minister from 12 December 2003 (until 14 December 2016). Before that, as governor (hokim), he had led Jizzakh region (1996–2001) and then Samarkand region (2001–2003). In other words, when he came to power he was one of the country's most experienced officials, a long-serving prime minister.

The December 2016 election

A snap presidential election was held on 4 December 2016. According to the results of the Central Election Commission, Shavkat Mirziyoyev won with roughly 88.6% of the vote. He was inaugurated on 14 December 2016. (International observers, including the OSCE, had assessed the election as lacking genuine competition.)

The turn to reform ("New Uzbekistan")

After Mirziyoyev came to power, the country took a series of reform steps. A decree signed on 5 September 2017 liberalized the exchange rate and made the som freely convertible for current transactions — ending the "black market." The Soviet-era "exit-visa" sticker required for citizens to travel abroad (beyond the CIS) was abolished from 1 January 2019. In 2016–2019 many political prisoners and detained journalists were released; the notorious Jaslyk prison was closed in August 2019. Measures against forced labor in the cotton harvest led the International Labour Organization to record a significant decline, and in March 2022 the Cotton Campaign ended its global boycott. Relations with neighbours also improved: state visits to Kyrgyzstan (September 2017) and Tajikistan (March 2018) reopened long-closed borders.

The Strategy of Actions

The main document of the reforms was the "Strategy of Actions on Five Priority Development Directions of Uzbekistan for 2017–2021," adopted in February 2017 (by a presidential decree of 7 February). This document set out directions of reform in state governance, the rule of law, the economy, the social sphere and foreign policy.

Important note

This is historical, fact-based information. The dates and figures (death — 2 September, burial — 3 September, election — 4 December, vote — ~88.6%) are drawn from reputable sources. The official explanation of the succession (Yuldashev proposing Mirziyoyev) rests on the official statement and is not an independently verified assessment.
