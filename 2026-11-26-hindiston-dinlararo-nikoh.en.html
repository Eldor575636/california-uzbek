Growing hurdles haunt India’s interfaith couples

The Special Marriage Act’s 30-day public notice and anti-conversion laws expose Hindu–Muslim couples to threats from families and vigilantes.

26 November 2026 · 1 min read · California Uzbek

What this is about

According to The Diplomat (25 November 2026), interfaith — especially Hindu–Muslim — couples in India face growing pressure from relatives and from legislation. Fewer than 3% of marriages in India are interfaith.

The Special Marriage Act

The Special Marriage Act, 1954 allows marriage without religious conversion, but requires couples to give 30 days’ notice to the marriage officer and to have the notice publicly displayed. Some groups monitor registry notice boards, circulate the notices online and alert families — exposing couples to harassment and threats.

Anti-conversion laws

Per the US Commission on International Religious Freedom (USCIRF), at least 13 Indian states have laws regulating religious conversion. Since 2017, Uttar Pradesh, Madhya Pradesh, Maharashtra and others added provisions aimed at conversion through marriage — popularly called ‘love jihad’ laws. In Uttar Pradesh penalties reach 10 years, and after a 2024 amendment up to life in some cases.

A recent case

In Maharashtra, Shravani Rane (22) and Faiz Akbar Khan (26) registered their marriage under the Special Marriage Act in October 2026. Rane’s mother filed a police complaint under the new Maharashtra religious-freedom law that took effect on 28 August 2026. The couple say no conversion occurred; on 16 November Rane rebutted the charges in court.

What an activist says

Once people believe the myth of ‘love jihad,’ real couples have to live with its consequences. — Ismat Ara, journalist and author of ‘Under a Saffron Sky’
