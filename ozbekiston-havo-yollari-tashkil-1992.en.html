The founding of Uzbekistan Airways (1992): the independent state's national airline

The national airline Uzbekistan Airways was established on 28 January 1992 and took over the operations of Aeroflot's Uzbekistan division on 31 May 1992. It was among the first carriers in the former USSR to switch to Western-built aircraft: in 1993 it acquired its first Airbus A310-300s and opened routes to Frankfurt and Bangkok. Its base is Tashkent (today Islam Karimov Tashkent International Airport). The fleet was later renewed with the Boeing 767 and then the Boeing 787-8 (first 787 in August 2016). The airline became one of the symbols of independent Uzbekistan's air links with the world. Based on official and open sources.

7 November 2026 · 2 min read · California Uzbek

Independence is built not only with a flag and a coat of arms, but with practical institutions that connect the state to the world. One such institution is a national airline. The founding of Uzbekistan Airways was one of the young state's first steps toward taking its direct air links with the world into its own hands. The following sets out this history using facts only.

Established: 28 January 1992

The national airline Uzbekistan Airways was established on 28 January 1992 — a few months after Uzbekistan declared independence. On 31 May 1992 it fully took over the operations and fleet of Aeroflot's division in Uzbekistan. In this way the country gained its own national carrier.

A Soviet inheritance: an old fleet

In the early years the airline's fleet consisted mostly of Soviet-built aircraft left over from Aeroflot. The new carrier faced two tasks: keeping domestic flights running without interruption, and at the same time modernizing the fleet to open international routes.

The switch to Western aircraft (1993)

In 1993 Uzbekistan Airways added its first Western aircraft — the Airbus A310-300 — a step that was among the first by any carrier in the former USSR. With the new aircraft the company was able to open long-haul routes such as Frankfurt and Bangkok. This connected the young state directly with Europe and Asia.

The fleet expands

The airline steadily renewed its fleet: Boeing 767-300ERs were ordered in late 1995, and the Boeing 757 and the Il-114 were later added. In August 2016 the airline received its first Boeing 787-8 Dreamliner. The main base for all international flights is Tashkent (today Islam Karimov Tashkent International Airport).

Why this matters in the history of independence

The founding of the national airline was a practical sign of building an independent state: managing its own airspace, international routes and links with the world independently. Today the airline also serves as a key air bridge to the homeland for millions of Uzbeks abroad — including the diaspora in the United States.
