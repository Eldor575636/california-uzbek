South Korea's car trade deficit with China hits a record

In January–October 2026 the deficit reached $4.23 billion; the main driver is a surge of Shanghai-built Teslas and Chinese electric vehicles.

22 November 2026 · 1 min read · California Uzbek

Although South Korea is one of the world's major carmakers, Chinese vehicles are taking an ever larger share of its market. According to the Korea International Trade Association (KITA), in January–October 2026 the country's car trade deficit with China reached a record $4.23 billion.

The figures

That is 92.7% higher than the $2.20 billion deficit a year earlier and already exceeds the figure for the whole of 2025. Car imports from China rose 79.2% to $4.39 billion, while Korea's car exports to China fell 34.9% to $168.77 million.

The main driver: electric vehicles

The key factor behind the deficit is electric vehicles. In this period 98,313 Teslas were newly registered in Korea — up 105% year-on-year — most of them assembled at Tesla's factory in Shanghai, China. The Model Y was the most-imported model, with 78,631 units sold. Tesla took more than 30% of Korea's imported passenger-car market.

Chinese brands

China's BYD sold more than 20,000 cars in Korea over the period, and Polestar also offered China-built electric vehicles. Overall, China overtook Germany in the first half of 2026 to become Korea's largest source of car imports.

The trend

The deficit has been widening year by year: $466 million in 2022, $943 million in 2023 and $1.73 billion in 2024. The data is based on figures from KITA, registration data from the Korea Automobile Importers & Distributors Association (KAIDA) and the Ministry of Trade, Industry and Resources, distributed by the Yonhap news agency.
