Is the Hijab Banned in Kazakhstan? The School Ban and the Secularism Debate

Short answer: there is no blanket ban — but in schools the hijab is prohibited under uniform rules. That restriction has sparked fierce debate over secularism and religious freedom in Kazakhstan.

15 September 2026 · 1 min read · California Uzbek

Is the hijab banned in Kazakhstan? Short answer: there is no general ban (in the street, at work or at university) — but in schools the hijab is prohibited under uniform rules. That restriction is what triggered fierce debate.

The basis for the ban

Kazakhstan's new Constitution states that the system of education and upbringing is secular (except in religious institutions). Nationwide uniform rules prohibit elements signalling religious affiliation.

The government's position

President Kassym-Jomart Tokayev defended secularism, stressing that a school is first a place to acquire knowledge and that religious belief is a private matter. He said Kazakhstan will remain a secular state while ensuring freedom of religion.

The pushback

The Spiritual Administration of Muslims of Kazakhstan (SAMK) opposed the ban and condemned stripping schools of their licences over headscarves.

According to Islamic Sharia, wearing a headscarf is an obligation (fard) for a Muslim woman who has reached puberty. A student's wearing of a headscarf should not prevent her from receiving an education.

— Spiritual Administration of Muslims of Kazakhstan (SAMK)

The human toll

Reports say that in the Atyrau region alone, 150 girls have left school since early September because of the ban (Education Ministry). Opponents argue it restricts freedom of conscience.

Context

The dispute reflects a wider regional question — the balance between secularism and religious freedom. Similar debates recur elsewhere in Central Asia.
