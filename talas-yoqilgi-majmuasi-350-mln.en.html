A $350 Million Fuel Processing Complex Is Being Built in the Talas Region

On Sept. 11, Kyrgyzstan's Ministry of Economy and the Kazakh company QAZAQ BITUM Processing signed a memorandum of intent.

11 September 2026 · 5 min read · California Uzbek

On Sept. 11, 2026, Kyrgyzstan's Ministry of Economy and Commerce and Kazakhstan's QAZAQ BITUM Processing signed a memorandum of intent on building a fuel processing complex in the Talas region. The project's estimated value is $350 million.

The document was signed by Kyrgyz Minister of Economy and Commerce Bakyt Sydykov and the Kazakh company's director, Yerzhan Beisebayev. President Sadyr Japarov attended the ceremony.

What the complex will produce

The project is intended to process straight-run gasoline and diesel fractions. The complex is also planned to produce polymer-modified bitumen (PG grade).

Bitumen is the primary material in road construction. Polymer-modified grades are more resistant to high and low temperatures and are therefore used for roads in mountainous areas.

  • Location: Talas region, Kyrgyzstan
  • Investment: ~$350 million
  • Investor: QAZAQ BITUM Processing (Kazakhstan)
  • Products: processing of gasoline and diesel fractions, polymer bitumen
  • Document type: memorandum of intent

Why dependence on fuel imports matters

Kyrgyzstan imports most of its petroleum fuel — mainly from Russia. This makes domestic prices dependent on external market conditions and delivery terms.

Rising world oil prices in 2026 were cited as one of the factors intensifying inflation in Kyrgyzstan: annual price growth stood at 11.7 percent as of Aug. 24.

Increasing domestic processing capacity is therefore one of the government's declared priorities. The official statement explained the project in terms of reducing import dependence, strengthening the Talas region's industrial potential and supporting the construction materials industry.

Economic ties with Kazakhstan

Kazakhstan is one of Kyrgyzstan's main trading partners, and both countries are members of the Eurasian Economic Union. Because customs barriers are low within the union, mutual investment projects are relatively easy to arrange.

Several foreign investment projects have been announced in Kyrgyzstan in recent weeks: the Sary-Tash logistics center near the Chinese border (up to $100 million) on Sept. 10, and on Sept. 13 the launch of the plant of the Altyn Tor gold project backed by Indian investment.

What is unclear

The signed document has the status of a memorandum. It does not specify construction timelines, the complex's annual capacity or the scheme for supplying raw materials.

The Talas region

Talas is a region in northwestern Kyrgyzstan bordering Kazakhstan. Its administrative center is the city of Talas.

Agriculture is the foundation of the region's economy; bean cultivation holds a particular place here, and most of the crop is exported.

Industrial capacity, by contrast, is relatively limited. Siting a large production complex there could mark a significant change for the region in terms of employment and tax revenues.

The project's products

The complex is intended to process straight-run gasoline and diesel fractions. This is the process of bringing intermediate products obtained from the primary refining of oil up to final fuel standards.

The second line is the production of polymer-modified bitumen. Bitumen is the primary binding material in road construction.

Polymer-modified bitumen is more resistant to high and low temperatures. For a country like Kyrgyzstan, with mountainous terrain and a sharply continental climate, this property carries practical significance, because road surfaces heat to 50 degrees in summer and freeze at sub-zero temperatures in winter.

Dependence on fuel imports

Kyrgyzstan imports most of its petroleum products. The main supplier is Russia; deliveries are made on preferential terms within the framework of the Eurasian Economic Union.

This dependence makes domestic prices sensitive to external factors. Rising world oil prices in 2026 were cited as one of the factors intensifying inflation in the country: annual inflation stood at 11.7 percent as of Aug. 24.

Increasing domestic processing capacity does not eliminate price volatility entirely, since the raw material is still imported. But it does make it possible to retain part of the value added within the country.

The Eurasian Economic Union framework

Kyrgyzstan and Kazakhstan are members of the Eurasian Economic Union. The union declares the free movement of goods, services, capital and labor.

In practice, this simplifies carrying out projects in Kyrgyzstan for Kazakh investors: customs formalities are simplified and technical regulations are shared.

Kazakhstan is one of Kyrgyzstan's main trading partners, and investment flows between the two countries have expanded in recent years.

September's wave of investment

The Talas project was one of several major initiatives announced in Kyrgyzstan in September 2026.

  • Sept. 2: transit trade agreement with Pakistan, targeting $200 million in trade
  • Sept. 10: memorandum on the Sary-Tash logistics center, up to $100 million in investment
  • Sept. 11: fuel complex in the Talas region, $350 million
  • Sept. 13: the plant of the Altyn Tor gold project came online, $34.2 million in investment

Most of these projects are at the memorandum or intent stage, and their implementation will depend on documents at the next stage.

Environmental aspects

Petroleum processing plants raise questions of air emissions, wastewater and waste storage. In international practice, an environmental impact assessment (EIA) document is required for such projects.

The official announcements gave no information on the project's environmental review or the terms for using water resources. Because the Talas region is primarily an agricultural area, this question may matter to local residents.

Kyrgyzstan's energy balance

Kyrgyzstan draws most of its electricity from hydroelectric plants; the largest is the Toktogul plant on the Naryn River. Fuel needed for heating and transport, however, is imported.

When reservoir levels fall in winter, electricity shortages arise and the country is forced to import from neighboring states. Energy security therefore holds a permanent place on the government's agenda.

In 2026, the region's states continued work on jointly financing the Kambarata-1 hydroelectric plant; Kyrgyzstan, Kazakhstan and Uzbekistan are taking part in the project.

Road infrastructure

Polymer bitumen production is directly connected to road construction. Roads are Kyrgyzstan's principal transport network, because the rail network is limited.

The roads connecting the country's south and north run over mountain passes and are sensitive to weather conditions in winter. The durability of road surfaces is therefore a practical concern.

The Sary-Tash logistics center announced on Sept. 10 is likewise intended for road freight flows; it sits on the Osh–Sary-Tash–Irkeshtam route.

No official information has also been released on the project's environmental review or the terms for using water resources. The Talas region is primarily an agricultural area.
