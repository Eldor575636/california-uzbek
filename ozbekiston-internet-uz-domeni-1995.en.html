The '.uz' domain and the arrival of the internet in Uzbekistan (1995–2002)

In the early years of independence Uzbekistan began building its own digital identity: on 29 April 1995 the national '.uz' domain was delegated by IANA, the first internet connections appeared in the mid-1990s, and in 1999 the state placed the single international gateway in the hands of UzPAK. In 2002 that monopoly was eased and UZINFOCOM was created. The article relies only on dates confirmed by two or more reliable sources.

27 November 2026 · 3 min read · California Uzbek

Having gained independence on 1 September 1991, Uzbekistan began to build its digital and telecommunications identity from scratch alongside its other state structures — previously all communications passed through Moscow. From the second half of the 1990s a national internet domain, the first connections and a state communications network took shape. Below, this process is set out in order, based only on dates confirmed by two or more reliable sources; where sources give different dates, this is noted separately (Source: IANA — .uz Delegation Record; Wikipedia — .uz).

The national '.uz' domain (1995)

Uzbekistan's national top-level domain '.uz' was delegated by IANA (the Internet Assigned Numbers Authority) on 29 April 1995. This date is confirmed both by the IANA root database record and by other independent sources. According to the sources, the domain was initially delegated to an individual in Tashkent, and from 1995 to 2001 '.uz' names were registered free of charge; in 2001 the administration right was transferred to a commercial representative in Uzbekistan (Source: IANA — .uz Delegation Record; Wikipedia — .uz).

Later, after an appeal by Uzbekistan's communications agency, ICANN/IANA re-delegated the '.uz' zone to the national UZINFOCOM center. The date of this re-delegation is given differently by different sources: the agreement between UZINFOCOM and ICANN is dated 18 October 2002, the appeal to December 2002, and IANA's formal redelegation report to 10 April 2003. For that reason it is more accurate to describe the process as having taken place in the window of 'late 2002 to early 2003' rather than on a single day. Today the '.uz' zone is managed by the UZINFOCOM center in Tashkent (Source: Wikipedia — UZINFOCOM; IANA — .uz Delegation Record).

The first internet connections

The first permanent internet connection in Uzbekistan appeared in the mid-1990s — according to some sources in 1994 — and Uzbekistan, together with Kazakhstan, was among the first states in Central Asia to establish a permanent connection. Early connectivity developed largely with the help of Western programs — including the Eurasia Foundation/USAID, the Soros Open Society Institute, IREX and scientific-network initiatives. Because sources differ on the exact first year, it is generalized here as 'the mid-1990s' (Source: RFE/RL — How The West Helps Five Nations Access The Internet).

UzPAK and the single-gateway monopoly (1999)

On 5 February 1999, by Resolution No. 52 of the Cabinet of Ministers, UzPAK — the 'National Network of Information Transmission' — was established. The same resolution gave UzPAK a monopoly over the country's single international internet gateway: all internet service providers (ISPs) were required to route international traffic through UzPAK. It should be clarified that the state did not hold a formal gateway monopoly in 1995–1998; the single-gateway regime was introduced specifically in 1999. Although there were dozens of providers in the country (by some estimates about 25 in 1999 and 44 in 2001), only the state gateway provided a legal connection to the global network (Source: OpenNet Initiative — Uzbekistan; International Crisis Group — Internet Censorship in Uzbekistan).

Easing the monopoly and UZINFOCOM (2002)

In October 2002, by Resolution No. 352 of the Cabinet of Ministers, 'On the Decentralization of Access to the World Computer Networks', UzPAK's single-gateway monopoly was formally abolished. In practice the liberalization was partial: most providers continued to route international traffic through UzPAK afterward. That same year — 2002 — the UZINFOCOM state enterprise was established by presidential decree under Uzbekistan's Agency for Communications and Informatization; it was this center that was later entrusted with managing the '.uz' domain zone (Source: OpenNet Initiative — Uzbekistan; Wikipedia — UZINFOCOM).

Number of users

In the 1990s the number of internet users was very small, and no reliable exact figures are documented for that period, so no specific number is given here. Data exists for the later period: by some estimates about 6.4% of the population in 2006, and by 2008 about 2.4 million people (8.8% of the population) used the internet. These figures are given only as context for the later period (Source: OpenNet Initiative — Uzbekistan).

Why this matters

The '.uz' domain, the first connections and the creation of the state gateway were part of independent Uzbekistan's process of separating from the Soviet/Russian system and building its own telecommunications and digital identity. This article relies only on dates confirmed by two or more reliable sources; where the sources disagree (the re-delegation date, the first-connection year) the information was deliberately given as a range or in cautious form, and no unverified figure was added.
