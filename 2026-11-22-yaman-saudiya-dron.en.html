Fuel station struck in Sanaa as Saudi Arabia downs a drone heading for Riyadh

The Houthis blamed the Saudi-led coalition for the strike on the capital; the same day the coalition intercepted and destroyed a Houthi drone heading toward Riyadh.

22 November 2026 · 1 min read · California Uzbek

Violence in Yemen's war has escalated again. On the morning of Sunday 22 November 2026, a fuel station belonging to a state oil company on 60th Street in the capital Sanaa was struck. The same day, the Saudi-led coalition said it had shot down a Houthi drone heading toward the Riyadh region.

The attack in Sanaa

According to Anis al-Asbahi, spokesman for the Houthi-run Ministry of Health, six employees were injured and material damage was caused; no deaths were reported. The Houthi authorities claim the strike was carried out by Saudi-led coalition aircraft. The coalition has not confirmed or claimed the strike — so the question of who carried out the attack remains at the level of each side's statements and is not independently verified.

Saudi Arabia's interception

The spokesman for the Saudi-led coalition, Major General Turki al-Maliki, said coalition air defences had intercepted and destroyed a drone launched by the Houthis toward the Riyadh region. His remarks were posted on social media platform "X" and confirmed by the Saudi Press Agency (SPA). The coalition reported no casualties or damage.

By the numbers

Six employees were injured in the Sanaa strike. One drone intercepted over the Riyadh region is the firmly confirmed figure; some outlets report further projectiles intercepted over the previous 24 hours, but exact counts differ between sources. According to AFP, around 26 people have been killed in Saudi Arabia in Houthi attacks since September, about 23 of them in attacks on airports.

Context

This strike is the latest in the ongoing clashes between the Houthis and Saudi Arabia. It is distinct from the October attacks on Riyadh airport, a 13 October strike on a different oil facility, and a 5 November ballistic missile fired toward Madina — it is a new 22 November event. Mutual blame has become routine, and claims about each strike are often not independently verified.
