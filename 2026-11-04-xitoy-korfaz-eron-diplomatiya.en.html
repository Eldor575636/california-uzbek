US–Iran tensions open a door for China in the Persian Gulf

On November 3, Beijing held back-to-back meetings with Iran, Oman and Saudi Arabia. The Guardian reports that America's confrontation with Iran is letting China expand its regional influence.

4 November 2026 · 2 min read · California Uzbek

As the prospect of renewed US military action against Iran grows after the midterm elections, China is stepping up its diplomatic influence in the Persian Gulf and the wider Middle East. On November 3, 2026, Beijing hosted back-to-back high-level meetings. The Guardian frames this as America's confrontation with Iran giving China an opening to position itself as a regional mediator — an analytical assessment, not a settled outcome.

The Beijing meetings

President Xi Jinping received Oman's Sultan Haitham bin Tariq on a state visit; according to Al Jazeera and The Guardian, this was the first visit to China by an Omani head of state since diplomatic ties were established (around 1978). Foreign Minister Wang Yi met Ali Bagheri Kani, deputy secretary of Iran's Supreme National Security Council, and — per The Guardian — Saudi national security adviser Musaad bin Mohammed Al Aiban. China announced that its Middle East special envoy will visit Iran, Oman and Saudi Arabia this month for de-escalation talks.

What are 'Trump's troubles with Iran'?

According to The Guardian, this refers to the joint US–Israeli strikes on Iran in February 2026, continuing tensions over the Strait of Hormuz, and President Donald Trump's threats to resume action against Iran after the midterm elections. The Washington Post described it as "the threat of renewed U.S. war with Iran."

Oil and interests

According to The Guardian, China buys roughly 90% of Iran's crude oil exports; the same outlet reports that China cut crude imports by about 30% in April–June 2026, with June falling to a decade low. These figures appear only in The Guardian and are not independently confirmed, so they are presented as the outlet's reporting rather than established fact.

Context and Xi's words

China brokered a restoration of ties between Saudi Arabia and Iran in 2023; The Guardian notes its regional diplomatic impact has been "limited" since. Attention is focused on an anticipated Trump–Xi meeting on the sidelines of the APEC summit in China in mid-November (the exact date varies across sources). At the meeting Xi said: "China has always been actively committed to stopping conflict and promoting peace, and hopes to contribute wisdom and solutions to resolving the regional security dilemma." The geopolitical assessments in this article are attributed to the sources; events are still developing.
