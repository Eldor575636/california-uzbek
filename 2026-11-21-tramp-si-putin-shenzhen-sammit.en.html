Trump, Xi and Putin meet in Shenzhen: the first-ever trilateral nuclear summit

On 19 November the leaders of three nuclear powers sat at one table for the first time; they issued a joint statement rejecting nuclear war, but signed no concrete arms-cut deal.

21 November 2026 · 1 min read · California Uzbek

On 19 November 2026, US President Donald Trump, Chinese leader Xi Jinping and Russian President Vladimir Putin met in the Chinese city of Shenzhen. The meeting took place on the sidelines of the APEC summit at a state residence and lasted about 90 minutes. International media described it as the first-ever trilateral meeting of the leaders of three nuclear powers.

The joint statement

A joint statement was issued at the end of the meeting. In it the three states reaffirmed that a nuclear war cannot be won and must never be fought, and agreed to continue dialogue on reducing nuclear risks, preventing proliferation and maintaining strategic stability. Stabilising supplies of refined petroleum products and reforming the UN were also mentioned.

Modest outcomes

Observers judged the outcomes modest. China's own statement was more cautious than the US framing: it said the leaders agreed to discuss reducing nuclear risks and preventing proliferation, but there was no concrete, binding cut. According to Trump, 'The United States, Russia and China are uniquely positioned to discuss how to reduce the number of nuclear weapons and to lessen the risk that those weapons will ever be used.'

Regional reaction

Pakistan welcomed the consensus as validation of its own warnings about the danger of confrontation between nuclear-armed states; this is Islamabad's reaction, not an outcome of the summit itself. For the states of Central Asia, stability in relations between Washington, Beijing and Moscow carries significant weight.
