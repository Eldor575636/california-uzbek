AfD leader Alice Weidel calls Islam 'Sharia', rejecting Le Pen's view

The AfD co-leader claimed on a Die Welt podcast that Islam and Sharia cannot be separated and dismissed France's Marine Le Pen's view that Islam is compatible with the Republic; Politico reported.

27 November 2026 · 1 min read · California Uzbek

Alice Weidel, co-leader of Germany's far-right Alternative für Deutschland (AfD), has equated the religion of Islam with a political and legal order. According to Politico Europe, Weidel made the remarks on a weekly video podcast produced by the German newspaper Die Welt.

What was said

In Weidel's claim, Islam and Sharia cannot be separated: 'Islam is Sharia and Sharia is Islam,' she said, adding that Islam is 'not merely a religion but a social order.' With this she rejected a view expressed in October by Marine Le Pen, leader of France's Rassemblement National, that Islam — as distinct from Sharia — can be compatible with the French Republic.

A rift between two far-right leaders

The exchange is a new stage in a long-running dispute between Europe's two largest hard-right parties. On 24 November Le Pen called for a 'Europe of nations' to replace a 'German Europe'; Weidel accused her of trying to win votes on anti-German sentiment. Le Pen's party sits in the European Parliament's Patriots for Europe bloc, while Weidel's AfD leads the rival Europe of Sovereign Nations (ESN) group.

Other statements

In the interview Weidel called for millions of people to leave Germany — more than the 2.6 million cited by AfD co-leader Tino Chrupalla. She described the AfD as 'the only and last protective power of Jewish life in Germany' and said that as chancellor she would fly to Russia and sit down with President Vladimir Putin.

The AfD's status

Germany's domestic intelligence service classified the AfD a 'confirmed right-wing extremist' body in May 2025; in February 2026 the Cologne Administrative Court provisionally suspended that label, so the federal party is currently a 'suspected' extremist case. Weidel herself said she expects an attempt to ban the party in 2027. The AfD won 20.8% in the February 2025 federal election, finishing second.
