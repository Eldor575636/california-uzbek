Pentagon spent $81.5m holding migrants at Guantanamo — for just 849 people

A Defense Department Inspector General report found the base, prepared for tens of thousands, held only 849 migrants in total and never more than 399 at once.

28 November 2026 · 1 min read · California Uzbek

A report by the US Defense Department Inspector General found the Pentagon committed about $81.5 million — precisely $81,525,406 — to transferring and holding migrants at Naval Station Guantanamo Bay (NSGB) in Cuba. The military carried out the mission at the request of the Department of Homeland Security (DHS); the operation began in 2025.

Why it is contested

The base was initially prepared to accommodate tens of thousands of migrants. Yet, according to the report, it held just 849 individuals over the whole period, and never more than 399 at any one time — a wide gap between built capacity and actual use.

The numbers

Between July and September, migrants stayed an average of seven days, with the longest individual stay reaching 102 days. As of September 2026, roughly 350 US military personnel were deployed at the base to support the DHS operation.

From the report

The document states that 'as of September 3, there were eight illegal aliens held at NSGB' — meaning that, despite the large outlay, almost no one remained at the base when the report was compiled.

Why it matters

The report sharpens the debate over the cost and effectiveness of immigration measures — a live issue for migrant communities in the US, including the Uzbek diaspora. An Inspector General report is an independent audit of government spending and raises questions about how taxpayer money was used.
