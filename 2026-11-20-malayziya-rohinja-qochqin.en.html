Malaysia's Rohingya: 133,000 refugees, but without the right to work or legal status

Malaysia has long been a haven for Rohingya Muslims, but because it has not signed the Refugee Convention they cannot legally work; in 2026 hate speech and pressure to return have intensified.

20 November 2026 · 1 min read · California Uzbek

Malaysia has for years been a de facto haven for Rohingya Muslims fleeing Myanmar. But in 2026 that role is eroding: anti-Rohingya hate speech has surged, some refugee schools have closed, and the government has stepped up repatriation operations.

Legal status

Malaysia has not ratified the 1951 Refugee Convention or its 1967 protocol. The UN refugee agency (UNHCR) issues refugee cards, but these do not grant legal status — Rohingya are treated as undocumented migrants with no formal right to work or study (Human Rights Watch, 3 August 2026).

By the numbers

According to Malaysia's home minister, as of 30 September 2026 there were 133,400 Rohingya registered with UNHCR in the country as refugees or asylum seekers. As of 31 October, 6,294 Rohingya were in immigration detention. Between January and October, 12,176 Myanmar nationals were repatriated (including 1,476 people on a Myanmar naval vessel on 29 September) — the minister stressed these figures do not specifically refer to Rohingya. France 24 reported that at least nine refugee schools had closed under threat and more than 100 people had been driven from their homes.

2026 developments

The government has taken over refugee registration from UNHCR, which halted its own registration. At the same time, mass repatriation operations are continuing.

Statements

Malaysia's Home Minister Saifuddin Nasution Ismail said: "This cooperation is important to ensure that repatriation operations can be carried out safely, systematically and responsibly, in accordance with existing laws and procedures." Bryony Lau, Deputy Asia Director at Human Rights Watch, noted that Malaysia has for many years hosted refugees fleeing violence by the Myanmar military.

Context

These developments come against the backdrop of the International Court of Justice's 19 November 2026 ruling that Myanmar committed genocide against the Rohingya. This article focuses specifically on the situation of Rohingya in Malaysia.
