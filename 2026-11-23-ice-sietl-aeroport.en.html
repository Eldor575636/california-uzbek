ICE arrests two passengers at Seattle airport — governor calls it 'out of control'

Masked agents detained two people before boarding at SeaTac. Washington's governor sharply criticized ICE; DHS called it a targeted operation.

23 November 2026 · 1 min read · California Uzbek

Two passengers were arrested on the jet bridge at Seattle-Tacoma International Airport (SeaTac) at around 8:05 a.m. on Saturday, 21 November 2026, as they were about to board an Alaska Airlines flight to Los Angeles. The incident drew wide attention: Washington's governor sharply criticized federal immigration agents, while the federal government said it was a targeted operation.

What happened

Bystander video circulating on social media shows masked, plainclothes agents restraining the passengers on the boarding bridge. Alaska Airlines said the action was not previously known to it.

Reaction from state and city leaders

Washington Governor Bob Ferguson sharply criticized the agents, saying ICE is 'out of control' and questioning masked agents detaining passengers without identifying themselves. He said a state law he signed in March 2026 restricting law-enforcement officers from covering their faces may have been violated. Seattle Mayor Katie Wilson called the tactics 'unacceptable and un-American'. The Port of Seattle, which operates the airport, said it was 'outraged', had no advance knowledge, and opened an inquiry into how the agents accessed the bridge.

Federal response

In an official statement, the Department of Homeland Security (DHS) called the operation a targeted enforcement action to arrest Ricardo Larrondo Mendoza, who it said had overstayed his visa. According to DHS, the second person arrested, Sofia Hernandez Mejia, is charged with assaulting a federal officer; DHS alleged she threw hot coffee on officers. These allegations are DHS's account and have not been independently confirmed.

Context

The incident came amid broader disputes over US immigration operations in 2026. The names of those arrested and the allegations against them were released by DHS; state officials said they were examining the details of the operation.
