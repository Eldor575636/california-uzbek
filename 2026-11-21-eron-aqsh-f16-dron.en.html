Iran claims it fired a missile at a US F-16 over the Gulf; CENTCOM denies it

Iran's army said it hit a US F-16 over the Gulf on 20 November; US Central Command denied the claim. The accounts have no independent confirmation.

21 November 2026 · 1 min read · California Uzbek

According to a claim by Iran's army, on the evening of Friday 20 November 2026 Iranian air defences fired a missile at a US F-16 fighter jet over the Gulf. The Iranian account says the pilot aborted the mission and was forced to make an emergency landing at one of the nearest airports in the region. All details of the attack are Iran's claim.

The drone claim over Qeshm Island

Earlier, on 14 November, Iran's Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) said it had shot down a US MQ-1 drone over Qeshm Island in the Strait of Hormuz using a newly developed air-defence system. The IRGC navy also claimed it had forced down a US drone using electronic warfare. These claims were made by official Iranian sources.

The US denial

US Central Command (CENTCOM) firmly denied the F-16 claim, stating: 'Iran did not attack a US F-16 fighter aircraft yesterday — at all', and adding that 'All US fighter jets in the Middle East are safe and accounted for.' CENTCOM did not separately comment on the Qeshm Island drone claim.

No independent confirmation

The Reuters and Al Jazeera news agencies noted that no independent confirmation was presented to support Iran's claim that an aircraft had been hit. During the ongoing war Iran has previously made similar claims of downing or damaging US aircraft, which the US military has denied. For that reason the kinetic details of the incident are treated as unverified claims.
