South Korea seeks to more than triple funding for a Koryo-saram museum in Tashkent

The Overseas Koreans Agency has proposed raising the museum's funding from $1.89m to $6.44m for 2027. The opening is timed to the 90th anniversary of the 1937 deportation.

24 November 2026 · 1 min read · California Uzbek

South Korea's Overseas Koreans Agency (OKA) plans to build a museum in the Uzbek capital Tashkent dedicated to the history of the Koryo-saram — the ethnic Koreans of Central Asia. According to The Times of Central Asia, the 2027 draft budget proposes a substantial increase in funding for the museum.

How much funding

The Times of Central Asia reports that funding for the museum is proposed to rise from $1.89 million in 2026 to $6.44 million in 2027 — an increase of 239.7%. Part of the money would go directly to construction. The project proceeds under an intergovernmental agreement between Korea and Uzbekistan.

Where the museum will be built

The museum will be built on the grounds of the House of Korean Culture and Arts in Tashkent; Uzbekistan has provided a 30,000-square-metre site. The plan includes a 478-seat auditorium. The museum will document around 160 years of Korean history in Central Asia, including the 1937 deportation.

Why 2027

The opening is timed for 2027 — the 90th anniversary of the 1937 deportation and the 35th anniversary of diplomatic relations between Korea and Uzbekistan. However, The Times of Central Asia notes that the construction contract has not yet been awarded and the 2027 budget still awaits parliamentary approval, which could make completion on schedule difficult.

Who the Koryo-saram are

The Koryo-saram are the ethnic Koreans of the former Soviet Union. Their ancestors migrated to the Russian Far East and were forcibly deported to Central Asia — mainly Uzbekistan and Kazakhstan — under Stalin in 1937. That year 171,781 Koreans were deported; by 1938 some 74,000 had settled in Uzbekistan. Today around 170,000 Koryo-saram live in Uzbekistan.
