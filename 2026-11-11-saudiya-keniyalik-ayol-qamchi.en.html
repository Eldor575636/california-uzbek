Kenyan domestic worker sentenced to 100 lashes in Saudi Arabia

According to court documents reviewed by The New York Times, a 40-year-old Kenyan woman was sentenced in Riyadh to 100 lashes and one year of banishment for giving birth outside marriage; Saudi authorities did not confirm the sentence.

11 November 2026 · 1 min read · California Uzbek

A Kenyan domestic worker has been sentenced in Saudi Arabia to 100 lashes and one year of banishment. The New York Times reported this based on court documents it reviewed (reporters Abdi Latif Dahir and Vivian Nereim, 10 November). Esther Wanja Muthoni, 40, a mother of five, was sentenced by a three-judge criminal court in Riyadh on a charge of 'sex outside marriage' — a crime under Saudi law — after she gave birth. The child was born in August 2025; the ruling came in October 2026.

How the case began

According to the reporting, the woman had come to Saudi Arabia legally, as a domestic worker through a recruitment firm. She had no lawyer during the proceedings — only a court translator. The child's father, an Egyptian resident of the kingdom, has since left the country. Whether the sentence has been carried out is unclear; the woman's documents have expired, blocking her return to Kenya.

Flogging and the 2020 reform

Saudi Arabia's Supreme Court announced in 2020 that it was ending flogging as a discretionary (ta'zir) punishment, presenting it as a reform. But that change did not cover 'hudud' punishments prescribed under the court's interpretation of Islamic law — including the penalty for sex outside marriage. So this case does not formally contradict the 2020 announcement, but it falls outside its limits.

Reaction and caveat

Maryam Aldossari of ALQST, a UK-based Saudi human-rights organisation, condemned the sentence, saying: 'It just shows the hypocrisy behind so-called Saudi reform — they say they empower women, yet the court sentences a mother to 100 lashes.' Kenya's Foreign Affairs Ministry also took note of the case. An important caveat: the sentence has not been confirmed by Saudi authorities — all details rest on court documents reviewed by The New York Times and on statements by rights advocates.
